Greek Sing has a hard deadline. If a backdrop isn't finished, the show goes on anyway, and our production of Shrek the Musical had 6 separate scenes that each needed their own set.

As Head Crew Coordinator, I led a 15-person crew that built and managed 11 stage backdrops for a live performance with 60+ stage members and an audience of 500+. Building the sets was difficult, but getting every scene change down to 5 to 10 seconds, reliably enough to hold up during a live show, was the bigger challenge. I planned where every piece would sit and how it would move, then ran the crew through 3 full run-throughs before we ever performed in front of an audience.

Even that preparation didn't cover everything. During tech week, the sets ramp turned out to be narrower than we'd planned around, which meant the path we'd rehearsed for moving pieces on and off stage no longer worked. I reassigned crew positioning and adjusted the movement plan in real time rather than rehearsing the fix in advance, and it held, with <mark>zero missed transitions or set breakdowns</mark> on performance night. The show placed 3rd in the competition.
