Greek Sing runs on a fixed date. There's no pushing the show back if a backdrop isn't finished — the curtain opens whether the set is ready or not, and our production was Shrek the musical, with 6 separate scenes that each needed their own look.

As Head Crew Coordinator, I led a 15-person crew building and managing 11 individual stage backdrops in support of a live performance with 60+ stage members, in front of an audience of 500+. The hard part wasn't the building — it was getting every scene change down to 5–10 seconds, across all 6 transitions, reliably enough to survive a live show. I mapped placement and movement logistics and ran the crew through 3 full run-throughs before we ever had an audience watching.

Even that preparation didn't cover everything. During tech week, the sets ramp turned out to be narrower than we'd planned around, which meant the path we'd rehearsed for moving pieces on and off stage no longer worked. I reassigned crew positioning and adjusted the movement plan in real time rather than rehearsing the fix in advance — and it held: <mark>zero missed transitions or set breakdowns</mark> on performance night. The show placed 3rd in the competition.
