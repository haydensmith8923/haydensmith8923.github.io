## What I would do differently

This was my first DCF and my first stock pitch, and I made plenty of mistakes. I built the model from scratch, though, and I learned more from getting things wrong in it than I would have from filling in a template.

The first thing I would change is the terminal growth rate. I used 3% in the base case and 3.25% in the bull case, which is on the aggressive end since both sit above long-run real GDP growth. Something closer to 2 to 2.5% would have been easier to defend. It mattered a lot, too, because the terminal value made up about 84% of my base case enterprise value.

The second is the recommendation itself. For a stock with a 1.5 beta, roughly 16% upside barely clears the 13.25% WACC I was discounting at, which leaves very little margin of safety. Pitching a volatile stock with that thin a cushion was riskier than I realized at the time.

The third is that my operating assumptions didn't match how strongly I believed my own thesis. I was confident in vertical integration, HASTE demand, and Neutron, but too much of my valuation came from the terminal value instead of from those drivers. Within a few months, RKLB traded well above my $49.60 target as Neutron progressed and the backlog and HASTE demand kept growing. The stock's run doesn't prove the model right, but it lined up with the parts of the thesis I believed in most, and my forecast should have reflected that conviction.
