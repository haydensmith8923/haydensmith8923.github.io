## What I would do differently

Building this again, the first thing I would add is a sensitivity table across WACC and perpetual growth. In the base case, the present value of the terminal value is roughly $20.4B out of a $24.3B enterprise value, so about 84% of the answer comes from assumptions about what happens after the forecast window. That deserved to be shown to the audience rather than buried in a cell.

The second is gross margin. All three scenarios expand margin to somewhere between 74% and 80% by FY2031, which is aggressive for a business that builds and launches physical hardware. I believe the direction is right given vertical integration, but the magnitude needed a defense I did not have ready at the time.

The third is that my bear case was not bearish enough. It still has Rocket Lab reaching profitability and compounding to billions in operating income by 2031. That tests how fast the thesis works, not whether it is wrong. A real downside case should have modeled a Neutron failure, not a Neutron delay.
