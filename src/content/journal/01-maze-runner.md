If you haven't seen it, The Maze Runner starts with a kid named Thomas waking up in a field called the Glade with no memory of who he is. The Glade is surrounded by a giant maze, and he's stuck there with a group of other teenage boys who can't remember anything either. The first movie is mostly about figuring out the maze and getting out. It's a fun movie, but the two sequels are where the series got good for me.

The second movie, The Scorch Trials, introduces the Flare, a virus that slowly destroys your brain until you turn violent. It also ends with Teresa betraying everyone. She was the only girl in the Glade and one of the people Thomas trusted most, and she tips off WICKED, the organization that put them in the maze, because she believes WICKED can use the immune kids to find a cure. What makes it hurt is that she really believes she's doing the right thing, and she's willing to lose Thomas to do it.

Newt is a completely different situation. He's been Thomas's best friend since the Glade, the calm one who keeps the group together, and in The Death Cure it turns out he isn't immune to the Flare. He spends most of the movie getting worse and trying to hide it for as long as he can. Nobody betrays anybody. The virus just takes him a little at a time, and he knows exactly what's happening to him. His last scene with Thomas is the hardest part of the whole trilogy to watch, and the letter he leaves behind made it even worse.

That's why the third movie stuck with me. One friend breaks Thomas's trust on purpose, and the other is taken by something neither of them could stop. For a series that started with teenagers running through a maze, the ending is a lot sadder than I expected, and I liked it more because of that.
