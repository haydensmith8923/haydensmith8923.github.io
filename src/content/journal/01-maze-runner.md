I came to this series for the maze and stayed for what happens after they get out of it, which is not what I expected going in. The first film is a puzzle box. By the third one it has turned into something sadder and more interesting, and almost all of that is down to two characters.

Teresa is the betrayal. She hands Thomas over, and the reason the whole thing stings is that she isn't a villain about it. She has decided the cure matters more than the person, and she believes it, and she is willing to lose him to be right. That is a more uncomfortable kind of betrayal than a straightforward heel turn. There's no moment where she cackles. She just quietly decides the math works out and acts on it, which is the version that actually happens to people.

Newt is the other thing entirely, and he's the reason the third film lands as hard as it does. Newt doesn't choose anything. The Flare takes him, and it takes him slowly, and the worst part is that he knows it's happening while it happens. He asks Thomas for help before he's gone, and then he isn't himself anymore, and Thomas has to watch someone he'd have died for turn into something that wants to hurt him while still wearing his face. There's nothing to fight and nothing to outrun. The plea at the end of that sequence is the single best scene in the trilogy and it isn't close.

What I liked is that those two losses sit side by side. One person is taken by something nobody controls. The other one walks away on purpose and thinks she's doing the right thing. Putting both in the same story changes what the ending is about. It stops being a series about escaping an enemy and becomes a series about carrying what you couldn't prevent, which is a heavier place to land than most films aimed at this audience are willing to go.
