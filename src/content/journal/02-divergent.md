The premise is the whole appeal here. Divergent builds a society that sorts every person into exactly one quality, brave or smart or selfless or honest, and then organizes all of life around that one label. Tris doesn't come back clean on the test. She's more than one thing, which the system has no category for, and the entire plot follows from her refusing to pretend otherwise.

I find that setup more interesting than most dystopian movies because the real villain is the sorting system itself. A system that needs everyone to fit in one box is eventually going to treat the people who don't fit as a threat. Jeanine is scary because she truly believes society would fall apart without the factions, and that anyone who doesn't fit has to go.

Tris is the reason the movie works for me. She leaves Abnegation, the faction she grew up in, to join Dauntless, gets beaten up over and over in initiation, and still refuses to pretend she's only one thing, even when hiding it would keep her safe. I respect that a lot, and it's why I kept rooting for her through the whole series.

Four is a big part of it too. He's her instructor in Dauntless, and it turns out he's hiding things of his own, like his real name and the family he left behind in Abnegation. Their relationship works because they're the two people in the movie who can stop pretending around each other. Honestly, they're the main reason I still rewatch the first movie, even though the later ones get messier.
